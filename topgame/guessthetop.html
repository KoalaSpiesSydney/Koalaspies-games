Guess the Top 5: a free team guessing game

Guess the Top 5 is a free game show for two to four teams, played together on one screen. Each round hides a secret question behind a one-word topic. The top answers are hidden on a board, and the teams take turns guessing what is on it. If you have played a Family Feud–style survey game, you will pick it up in seconds.

How to play

  1. Split into two to four teams and give each team a name.
  2. On your turn, catch one of the disco balls as it floats past. Each one holds a topic with a secret question.
  3. The answers are hidden on the board. For “the most” questions it is a ranked top 5 and higher answers score more points. For “name 5” questions, any 5 correct answers count.
  4. The team that chose guesses first and keeps going while they are right. Every round has a 2-minute timer.
  5. A wrong guess is a strike. The first team to shout “We’ll take it!” takes over. Two strikes and you are out for that round.
  6. In Fast Money, the leading team writes five quick questions, and two players from the other team race the clock for double points.
  7. Each team has one appeal per game: play the yellow card to dispute an answer and check it online. Win the appeal and you keep it; lose and it’s gone.
  8. Otherwise, no phones and no asking AI during the game. The team with the most points wins.

Great for

  • ESL and English classes: fast speaking practice, topic vocabulary and a reason to argue politely.
  • Classrooms: brain breaks, end-of-term games and revision with your own questions.
  • Family game night and parties: quick to learn, loud to play, and as good with four people as fourteen.
  • Team building: an icebreaker for workshops, staff meetings and online calls.

What you need

One screen everyone can see, such as a laptop, a projector or a TV, and two to four teams. There is nothing to download, no account to make and no cost. It runs in the web browser on computers, tablets and phones.

Questions

Is Guess the Top 5 free?
Yes. It is free to play, with no sign-up.
Does every player need a phone?
No. The whole group plays on one shared screen, and phones stay away during the game.
How long does a game take?
Each round lasts up to 2 minutes. You choose how many topics each team picks, so a game can be a quick ten-minute warm-up or a full lesson.
Can I use it to teach English?
Yes. Teams have to agree on answers out loud, which makes it a lively speaking activity for ESL classes at most levels.
Can we play online?
Yes. Share your screen on Zoom or Teams and let the teams call out their answers.
